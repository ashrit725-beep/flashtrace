Limitations

  • Headless sRGB rendering is not a physical display; brightness, gamma and viewing distance differ per user.
  • Canvas/WebGL attribution is element-level only (what is drawn inside cannot be attributed).
  • Cross-origin iframes are unattributable and cannot be clock-controlled.
  • Animated GIF/WebP/APNG, animated SVG images and Web Workers force non-deterministic capture (strategy C) and a PASS becomes INCONCLUSIVE.
  • Under realtime capture (strategy C) a measured rate can be one flash per second higher or lower than the page's, because a compositor frame drawn late or dropped moves a transition by one frame.
  • Under strategy A, work that the page starts and the browser finishes on the real clock is not waited for (ADR-052): React scheduler tasks (MessageChannel messages), the page's handling of `fetch` and `XMLHttpRequest` answers, history traversals (`history.back()`, `forward()`, `go()`), script, style and image load events, `window.postMessage`, WebSocket and EventSource messages, storage callbacks and Navigation API traversals. A page that changes what it shows on such an event can show that change one frame earlier or later in another capture (several frames when the page also holds the change behind a gate on its own timers), although both captures are reported as deterministic.
  • Under strategy A, IntersectionObserver is FlashTrace's own implementation (ADR-052). It clips a target by its ancestors' overflow along an approximated containing-block chain and ignores transforms, `clip-path`, `clip`, `border-radius`, `overflow-clip-margin` and `scrollMargin`, so a reveal that depends on those can start at a different scroll position than in a browser; `isVisible` (`trackVisibility`) equals `isIntersecting`, without an occlusion test.
  • The fine-pattern exception (T6) and spatial-pattern hazards are not evaluated.
  • Only the captured viewport and duration are analysed; content revealed by scrolling or interaction is analysed only if an interaction script drives it.
  • Window size for viewports other than 1024 px wide is scaled by the width ratio (an assumption).
  • The 341×256 px window is scored in whole 4×4 px cells and slides in 4 px steps, so a flashing area wider or taller than the window can be under-measured by up to 3 px along such an edge.
  • No numbers in this repository are estimates: accuracy, agreement and runtime figures exist only in `docs/RESULTS.md`, produced by `flashtrace bench`, with date and commit hash.
  • Math.random was replaced by a fixed-seed generator for this capture, but crypto.getRandomValues was not, so content driven by it may differ between runs (ADR-022).
  • Flashing faster than 30 per second (half the 60 fps capture rate) cannot be seen in the frames (ADR-019).

Assumptions

  • Red flashes were judged with the WCAG 2.2 working definition: one state has R/(R+G+B) >= 0.8 and the two states differ by more than 0.2 in CIE 1976 u'v' chromaticity (ADR-001).
  • The redness ratio R/(R+G+B) is computed on each 4x4 px cell's colour re-encoded to whole 0-255 sRGB values, and is 0 when R < 16: a red that dark has no visible hue (ADR-003, ADR-020).
